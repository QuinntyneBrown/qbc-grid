# L1 - High-Level Requirements

## Product

`qbc-grid` is an Angular library that lays out dashboard tiles on a cell-based grid
and lets a user move and resize those tiles with a pointer or the keyboard. It targets
Open MCT style operator dashboards on large desktop screens.

The feature surface is deliberately tiny. A capability that is not listed below is not
in the product, and "Out of Scope" names the omissions that a reader would otherwise
assume were oversights.

## Interpretations of the source prompt

`docs/prompt.md` is terse. Three readings were fixed here so that requirements,
design, and tests agree:

1. **"lock mode, edit or live mode"** is read as two grid modes - `live` and `edit` -
   plus a per-tile `locked` flag that survives edit mode. Two grid modes that both
   forbid interaction would be indistinguishable, so locking is placed on the tile,
   which is also where `gridster` and `gridstack` put it.
2. **"drag with shadow"** is read as two simultaneous affordances: the dragged tile is
   lifted under the cursor with an elevation shadow, and a separate shadow rectangle
   marks the cell the tile will land in.
3. **"showing the grid upon moving"** is read as a cell overlay that is drawn only for
   the duration of a move or resize.

## Out of Scope

Deliberately excluded, and therefore not defects:

- No collision resolution during interaction - no push, no swap, no float, no
  compaction. A tile lands where it fits or it does not land.
- No touch, pen, or mobile support, and no breakpoint-driven column collapsing.
- No backend, no HTTP, and no persistence inside the library. The grid emits layout
  data and accepts layout data; where that data is stored is the host application's
  decision. The demonstration application that hosts the acceptance tests stores a
  layout in the browser, which is test scaffolding rather than a product capability.
- No nested grids, no tile groups, no multi-select, no undo stack.
- No tile chrome, headers, menus, or content framework. Tiles are projected content.
- No animation system. Two transitions are permitted and nothing else moves: a fade on
  the interaction overlay, and a settle on a tile returning to its geometry after a
  refused or abandoned interaction.

## L1-001: Cell-based grid layout
The grid shall place tiles on a fixed number of equal-width columns derived from the
host container's width, position every tile by whole-cell coordinates and whole-cell
spans, and grow downward without limit.

## L1-002: Live and edit modes
The grid shall be in exactly one of two modes at any moment. In `live` mode the layout
is inert and no interaction affordance is presented. In `edit` mode unlocked tiles can
be moved and resized.

## L1-003: Locked tiles
An individual tile shall be markable as locked. A locked tile shall be immovable and
non-resizable even while the grid is in edit mode, and shall present no affordance that
suggests otherwise.

## L1-004: Move a tile by dragging
In edit mode the user shall be able to move an unlocked tile with the pointer. During
the drag the tile shall be lifted under the cursor and a shadow shall mark the cell
position the tile will occupy on release.

## L1-005: Resize a tile by dragging
In edit mode the user shall be able to resize an unlocked tile by dragging its corner
handle, in whole-cell increments, within the size limits declared for that tile.

## L1-006: Grid revealed during interaction
The cell structure of the grid shall become visible when a move or resize begins and
shall disappear when it ends.

## L1-007: Add and remove tiles
The host application shall be able to add a tile with an explicit position, add a tile
without a position and have the grid choose one, and remove a tile by id.

## L1-008: Layout is a serializable contract
The current layout shall be readable as plain data, shall be emitted whenever a user
interaction changes it, and shall reproduce the same visual result when supplied back
to the grid.

## L1-009: Supplied layouts are repaired, not trusted
A layout supplied to the grid shall be normalized deterministically into a valid state.
The grid shall not throw, hang, or render overlapping tiles because of malformed,
stale, or hostile layout data.

## L1-010: Keyboard operation
Every move and resize available to the pointer shall also be available from the
keyboard, and the resulting position shall be announced to assistive technology.

## L1-011: Desktop viewports only
The grid shall be correct and usable across large desktop viewports, shall fill the
width of its container, and shall not change its column count in response to width.

## L1-012: Interaction performance
Moving and resizing shall remain smooth on a realistically populated dashboard, without
per-pointer-event layout work or per-pointer-event output emissions.

## L1-013: Projected content only
Tile content shall be supplied by the host through Angular content projection. The
library shall not render host-supplied strings as markup.

## L1-014: Distributable, token-styled library
The grid shall ship as a standalone Angular library with no dependency on the
application that consumes it, and shall draw every colour, dimension, radius, shadow,
and duration from design-system custom properties.
